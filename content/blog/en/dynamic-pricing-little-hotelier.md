Dynamic pricing means changing your nightly rate with demand instead of keeping one price per season. For a small hotel on Little Hotelier, that comes down to four signals: how full you are, what comparable hotels nearby charge, what is happening in town, and how close the date is. You can do it by hand with a weekly routine, with Little Hotelier's own add-ons and partner tools, or with Hotelier Tools, which suggests a price per room and night, shows why, and writes it to Little Hotelier only when you confirm.

## What is dynamic pricing for a small hotel?

A big hotel has a revenue manager and a pricing system. A 12-room hostal has you, at the desk, between check-ins. For you, dynamic pricing is a small set of decisions made the same way every week:

- **Your own occupancy.** Few rooms left on a night means you can charge more. Many rooms left close to the date means you may need to move.
- **The market.** What comparable properties nearby charge for the same night.
- **Events.** Fairs, concerts, conferences and public holidays that fill your town.
- **Lead time and day of week.** Weekends, bridge holidays and the last few days before arrival behave differently.
- **Limits.** A floor price you never go below and a ceiling that keeps you credible.

The goal is not the highest price. It is the best mix of price and nights sold across the season.

## A simple manual method you can start this week

You can do all of this in Little Hotelier's inventory grid (**Front Desk > Inventory**), without any extra tool. Block 20 minutes once a week.

1. **Write down a floor and a ceiling per room type.** The floor covers your costs plus the margin you accept. The ceiling is the most a guest would reasonably pay on your busiest night.
2. **Look at the next 60 to 90 days of occupancy.** Mark nights that are filling faster than usual and nights that are empty.
3. **Check 4 to 6 real competitors** on Booking.com or Google Hotels for those nights. Compare the same kind of room, with a similar review score and cancellation policy.
4. **List the events** in your town: trade fairs, festivals, concerts, public holidays. Price those nights early, before the city fills.
5. **Adjust in small steps.** Raise filling nights, hold or nudge down empty ones, never past your floor or ceiling.
6. **Use restrictions, not just prices.** A minimum stay on event weekends, or closing arrivals on the busiest night so guests book the whole weekend, often does more than a price change. See [CTA, CTD, minimum stay and stop sell explained](/blog/little-hotelier-cta-ctd-stop-sell).
7. **Keep a log** of what you changed and whether the rooms sold. After a season you will know your own market better than any tool.

::callout{type="tip" title="Compare like with like"}
Many hotels charge more on Booking.com and Expedia than at the desk to cover the commission, and some prices you see are member or Genius discounts. So a competitor's Booking.com price is not their desk rate. Compare the same channel to the same channel, or adjust for your own commission. See [how much commission Booking.com takes](/blog/booking-com-commission).
::

## Which competitor prices matter?

Not every hotel in town is your competitor. Pick properties that a guest would really compare with you:

- the same area or a short walk away
- a similar category (within about one star)
- a similar review score
- the same kind of room: a double with a private bathroom is not a dorm bed

For hostels, Hostelworld prices matter as much as Booking.com. For city hotels, Google Hotels shows what guests see first when they search. Expedia and Agoda matter if guests from their markets book you.

## What Little Hotelier offers natively

Little Hotelier already has several pricing tools. As of October 2026:

- **Inventory grid and Bulk Update** (**Front Desk > Inventory**): change rates and restrictions for a date range, by weekday and room rate. This is where the manual method above happens ([inventory grid guide](https://helpcentre.littlehotelier.com/en/articles/8673827-front-desk-manage-rates-availability-and-restrictions-in-the-inventory-grid)).
- **Derived rates**: a rate can follow another by a percentage or an amount, so you move one base rate and the others follow ([overview of room rates](https://helpcentre.littlehotelier.com/en/articles/8840580-overview-of-room-rates)).
- **Competitor rates report** (Insights): pick up to ten competitors and see their Booking.com rates for up to 30 days ahead, refreshed every 24 hours. It shows data; it doesn't set prices ([competitor rates report](https://helpcentre.littlehotelier.com/en/articles/8673694-competitor-rates-report)). Availability depends on your plan.
- **Dynamic Revenue Plus**: Little Hotelier's revenue tool. Its [product page](https://www.littlehotelier.com/dynamic-revenue-plus/) describes pricing recommendations you can automate or apply by hand, competitor activity, holidays and local events, and daily alerts in the Little Hotelier app. It is sold as an add-on to your plan.
- **Partner pricing tools**: third-party revenue tools can connect too. RoomPriceGenie, for example, says its prices are pushed back into Little Hotelier automatically, including minimum stays ([RoomPriceGenie integration](https://roompricegenie.com/integrations/little-hotelier/)).

Which one fits depends on how much you want to hand over. Derived rates and the competitor report suit a hands-on owner. Dynamic Revenue Plus and partner tools automate more, for a monthly fee; ask each vendor for current prices.

## The faster way: recommendations with the reason, in Hotelier Tools

**Competitor Prices** (`/competitor-prices`) shows what nearby hotels charge each night next to your own Little Hotelier rate. ### Recommendations with "Why?"

**Recommendations** (`/price-recommendations`) shows, per room and night, today's rate next to a recommended one. Press **Why?** on any row to see the arithmetic step by step: where it started, how much the market pulled, which demand rules added or took off, which limit capped it, and which comparable hotels were used.

How a recommended price is built:

1. It **starts from your own rate** for that room and night, as it was before Hotelier Tools last changed it, so it never compounds its own changes.
2. **The market pulls it a little** towards the median of comparable hotels (within about one star and a similar guest score).
3. **Demand adds or takes off**: occupancy, scarcity (sold out +15%, two or fewer rooms left +10%), last minute, weekends, approved local events and people on your [waitlist](/blog/hotel-waitlist-sold-out-dates).
4. **Limits cap the move**: your minimum price, a ceiling against the dearest comparable hotel, a floor against the cheapest, and a maximum move against today's rate.
5. It is **rounded** to a price a guest would read.
6. **Date rules win**: a fixed price is written as typed, a frozen night keeps its rate, bounds keep it between two figures.

Competitor prices are OTA prices, so Hotelier Tools converts them using your own measured OTA markup before comparing. To apply, tick the nights (or use **Select all**), press **Apply to Little Hotelier…** and confirm. You can ask for an email with the changes.

### Rules in plain words

**Pricing rules** (`/pricing/settings/rules`) sets how much each signal is worth: how far to sit below the market, how strongly to follow it, what busy nights and events add, and the limits. You can also type a rule as a sentence, such as "1 € below the hotel across the street unless we are nearly full". The AI turns the sentence into a structured rule, and you approve it before it counts.

That is the only place AI touches the price logic, apart from finding events. The nightly price runs themselves are plain arithmetic: no AI model sits inside the loop.

For specific dates, **Date rules** (`/pricing/settings/dates`) lets you freeze a price, fix it, keep it between two figures, or set only a minimum stay.

### Local events

**Local events** (`/pricing/settings/events`) collects fairs, concerts, conferences and public holidays near the property. An AI scan searches month by month, and you can add events by hand. You approve or reject each detected event, and a rejection is remembered. An approved event lets prices rise above the market on its nights.

### Autopilot with guardrails

The **Autopilot** (`/pricing/settings/autopilot`) recomputes recommendations on a schedule and writes the changed rates to Little Hotelier for you. It is **off by default** and needs two things: the **Let the autopilot write prices to Little Hotelier** switch, and a scheduled task (the suggestion is every 3 hours, and it cannot run more often).

Its guardrails:

- each night moves at most **±20%** against the live rate by default
- never under your floor price
- only within a horizon you choose, 1 to 90 days ahead (14 by default)
- room types you exclude are never touched
- nights you parked on a "not for sale" rate are never touched
- fixed date rules are written exactly as typed
- an email lists every change with its reason, unless you switch it off

It writes minimum stays only if you separately allow it, and it **never** changes channel stop sell.

### The pricing log: what each change did

**History & outcomes** (`/pricing-log`) lists every rate written to Little Hotelier, by you, the autopilot or the assistant, with the rooms left then and now. After an increase it estimates extra revenue as rooms sold after the change times the difference. The page says plainly what that estimate cannot know, such as whether those rooms would have sold anyway. Treat it as a guide, not proof.

## How to start without risk

1. Set your **minimum price** per room type and your real rivals first.
2. Run recommendations for the next two weeks and read the **Why?** on a few nights. If the reasoning looks wrong, adjust the rules, not the price.
3. Apply by hand for a few weeks. Check the pricing log.
4. Only then consider the autopilot, with a short horizon and the change emails on.
